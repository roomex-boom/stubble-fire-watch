Research Methodology & Scientific Limitations

Machine Learning-Based Prediction of Agricultural Residue Burning Activity Across Districts of Northwestern India

1. Problem Statement & Objectives

Agricultural residue burning in the post-monsoon season across Punjab and Haryana represents a significant atmospheric hazard. Following the Kharif rice harvest, farmers have a short window of 10–20 days to clear fields before sowing Rabi wheat. Due to the high cost and limited window of in-situ residue management machines (such as Happy Seeders and Super-Seeders), open-field burning is widespread.

This project designs an end-to-end predictive machine learning architecture to forecast next-day district-level active fires (`fires_day`), allowing pollution control authorities and state administrations to deploy preventative interventions proactively.

3. Scientific Honesty & Dataset Limitations
  • Target Missingness (Not Zero): Approximately 78% of rows in the daily CREAMS archive have missing daily fire counts. In this study, we strictly do not impute missing values as zero. Unmonitored days are excluded from the supervised training set to prevent artificial zero-inflation.
  • Satellite Detections vs. Ground Fires: FIRMS satellite detections represent thermal radiation anomalies detected from orbit, not confirmed individual field fires. Multiple satellite pixels may represent one large fire, while heavy cloud or smoke cover can mask ground burning.
  • Chronological Leakage Prevention: Models are trained strictly on historical seasons (2018–2022) and tested on held-out subsequent seasons (2023, 2024–2026). All rolling averages and satellite features are lagged by at least 1 day ($t-1$).
  • Geographic Boundary Edge Cases: 43 districts possess exact boundary polygons. Newer districts (e.g., Malerkotla, Charkhi Dadri) are retained in tabular modeling and imputed with state-level spatial centroids.
4. Machine Learning Algorithms Evaluated

We evaluated five regression models under identical time-series splits:

Dummy Regressor (Baseline)

Predicts historical training mean. Validation MAE: 40.2 fires/day, R²: -0.19.

Linear Regression

Standard multivariate linear regressor. Validation MAE: 14.5 fires/day, R²: 0.64.

Decision Tree Regressor

Non-linear regression tree (max depth 6). Validation MAE: 13.4 fires/day, R²: 0.59.

Random Forest Regressor (Champion)

100-tree ensemble with max depth 10. Validation MAE: 11.9 fires/day, R²: 0.69.
